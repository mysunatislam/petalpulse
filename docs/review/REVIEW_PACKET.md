## Period pain: what’s common and what helps

id `period-pain` · hash `047b127b` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- ACOG FAQ046: Dysmenorrhea – Painful Periods
- NICE Clinical Knowledge Summaries: Dysmenorrhoea

### English: Period pain: what’s common and what helps

_Most period pain is common and can be eased at home. Here is when it is worth getting checked._

**What’s common**

Cramping in the lower belly, sometimes in the back or thighs. It usually starts just before or as bleeding starts and eases within 2 to 3 days. It happens because substances called prostaglandins make the womb tighten.

**What can help**

Heat on the belly or back, gentle movement or stretching, rest, and pain relief such as ibuprofen or paracetamol taken as the packet says. Ibuprofen works best when started early. Don’t take ibuprofen if you have a stomach ulcer, kidney problems or asthma that it makes worse, or ask a pharmacist first.

**When to get checked**

If pain keeps you from school or work, doesn’t ease with pain relief, gets worse over the months, or comes with pain during sex, when you poo, or between periods.

## When to see a doctor

### English: When to see a doctor

_Signs that need help today, and signs that are worth an appointment soon._

**Get help now**

Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for 2 hours or more. Fainting, or feeling you might faint. Sudden, severe pain on one side of the lower belly, especially if your period is late or you could be pregnant. Fever with pelvic pain or unusual discharge. In Bangladesh, call 999.

**Book an appointment**

No period for 3 months when you aren’t pregnant. Periods lasting more than 7 days. Bleeding between periods or after sex. Pain that is getting worse or stops daily life. Mood changes before your period that hurt your relationships, studies or work.

**Bring your notes**

A few cycles of notes make an appointment much easier. The health summary in Settings turns your logs into a short PDF for your doctor.

## Heavy periods

id `heavy-bleeding` · hash `3970e478` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- NICE guideline NG88: Heavy menstrual bleeding – assessment and management

### English: Heavy periods

_Heavy bleeding is common and treatable. What counts as heavy is about how it affects your life._

**What counts as heavy**

Changing a pad every 1 to 2 hours, needing two kinds of protection at once, passing clots bigger than about 2.5 cm, bleeding through clothes or bedding, or periods that last longer than 7 days.

**Why it matters**

Over time heavy bleeding can lower your iron, which can leave you tired, breathless or dizzy. It can also make it hard to go out, study or work, and that deserves care too.

**What a doctor may do**

Ask about your periods, check for anaemia with a blood test, and talk through treatments such as tranexamic acid, anti-inflammatory medicines or hormonal options.

## Irregular cycles

id `irregular-cycles` · hash `7b2061b7` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- FIGO systems for abnormal uterine bleeding (2018)
- ACOG Committee Opinion 651: Menstruation in girls and adolescents

### English: Irregular cycles

_Cycles vary more than most people think. Here is what’s usual, and when a change is worth checking._

**What’s usual**

For adults, cycles are usually 24 to 38 days long and can change by up to about a week from one to the next. In the first few years after periods start, cycles of up to 45 days are common.

**Common reasons for changes**

Stress, illness, big changes in weight or exercise, breastfeeding, starting or stopping hormonal contraception, thyroid problems and PCOS.

**When to get checked**

No period for 3 months when you aren’t pregnant, cycles that are often shorter than 24 or longer than 38 days, or a sudden change from your usual pattern.

## PCOS: the basics

id `pcos` · hash `5dde3241` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (2023)

### English: PCOS: the basics

_PCOS is common, affecting about 1 in 10 women. It is not your fault, and it is about more than weight._

**What it is**

A doctor diagnoses PCOS when at least two of these are found: ovulation that is irregular or missing (often irregular periods); signs of higher androgen hormones, such as extra facial or body hair, acne, or a blood test; and many small follicles on the ovaries on an ultrasound. The ultrasound isn’t used for teenagers in their first 8 years of periods.

**Living with it**

Treatment depends on what matters most to you: periods, skin, hair, mood or pregnancy. Gentle habits around food, movement and sleep can help, alongside medical care. Weight stigma helps no one.

**In this app**

If you’ve told us you have PCOS, phase estimates are shown with extra caution, because ovulation timing can vary a lot.

## Endometriosis: signs worth checking

id `endometriosis` · hash `966630a1` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- NICE guideline NG73: Endometriosis – diagnosis and management
- WHO fact sheet: Endometriosis (2023)

### English: Endometriosis: signs worth checking

_Endometriosis affects about 1 in 10 women and girls of reproductive age, and often takes years to be diagnosed._

**What it is**

Tissue similar to the lining of the womb grows outside the womb. It often causes painful periods and pelvic pain, and can affect fertility.

**Signs worth checking**

Period pain that affects daily life, pelvic pain at other times, deep pain during or after sex, pain when you poo or pee during your period, or trouble getting pregnant.

**What helps**

Note your symptoms over a few cycles (the Pain Passport does this) and take them to a doctor. Treatments include pain relief, hormonal treatment and sometimes surgery. Your pain is real, and it is fair to ask for a referral if it isn’t getting better.

## PMS and PMDD

id `pms-pmdd` · hash `2a1824b1` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- NICE Clinical Knowledge Summaries: Premenstrual syndrome
- RCOG Green-top Guideline 48: Management of Premenstrual Syndrome

### English: PMS and PMDD

_Mood and body changes before a period are common. When they are severe, there is a name for it, and help._

**PMS**

Many people notice mood changes, bloating, tender breasts or tiredness in the days before their period, easing once it starts.

**PMDD**

A severe form where mood symptoms such as deep sadness, anxiety, irritability or feeling out of control seriously affect life in most cycles. It is a recognised condition and it can be treated.

**What can help**

Tracking how you feel for at least two cycles, regular sleep, movement, and talking to a doctor about treatments, which may include certain antidepressants or hormonal options. If you ever think about harming yourself, reach out now. The Support screen lists who to call.

## Iron, tiredness and periods

id `iron` · hash `d31780f8` · updated 2026-10-22

Sources:
- WHO fact sheet: Anaemia
- WHO guideline: Daily iron supplementation in adult women and adolescent girls (2016)

### English: Iron, tiredness and periods

_Monthly bleeding uses up iron. Low iron is very common in South Asia, and easy to check._

**Why periods matter**

Each period, and especially heavy ones, uses up some of your iron. Low iron is one of the most common causes of anaemia among women and girls in South Asia.

**Signs**

Tiredness, looking pale, breathlessness, dizziness, headaches, or cravings for ice or things that aren’t food.

**What helps**

Iron-rich foods such as lentils, spinach, liver, fish, eggs and chickpeas, eaten with vitamin C (guava, lemon or amla) to help your body absorb it. Tea with meals makes it harder to absorb. A simple blood test can check your iron before you take supplements.
